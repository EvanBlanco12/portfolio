Task A- Portfolio Review #1 -https://rjmeade.github.io/
"Rick Meade"-Mechanical Engineering

-Navigability
Based off of Rick's portfolio it's easy to navigate because the homepage lists his engineering projects individually. For example, his projects named, "including the Unmanned Ground Vehicle, UAV Forge, Autonomous Fixed-Wing Aircraft, and Computer Vision Research". Any reader is able to select a specific project directly from the main page of his portfolio, instead of searching through long documents. 

-Reproducibility 
Rick's portfolio provides strong information for reproducing his engineering work. For example, his Unmanned Ground Vehicle project explains the design requirements, weight limitations, material selection, motor selection, CAD process, and simulations. He even explains how different materials and cutouts geometries were compared before even selecting the final design for his project. 

-Evidence of Reasoning 
Rick once again does a particularly good job in showing his engineering reasoning. In the UGV project he wrote about, he explains how a Pugh analysis was used to compare materials based on weight, manufacturing capabilities, and cost. In the UAV project, he uses performance data to compare motor configurations and explains why he selected motor was chosen.

-Professional Tone 
Rick's writing uses technical engineering terminology and focuses on these 5 things specifically, requirements, calculation, simulations, testing, and lastly design decisioning. For example, he discusses momentum impulse calculations, static load simulation, factory of safety, and material selection. This makes the portfolio appropriate for an engineering employer to check out his technical skills.

Task A- Portfolio Review #2 -https://tylerwisniewski.github.io/
"Tyle Wisniewski"-Mechanical Engineering

-Navigability
Tyler's portfolio also has a Projects section on the main page this allows the reader to quickly find his engineering accomplishments. Individual projects have their own page such as his Mechatronics projects and carbon fiber surfboard fin project. This makes it easy for an employer to location any specific type of engineering experiences.

-Reproducibility
Tyler provides detailed technical information in several of his projects. In his surfboard project he explains the design requirements, CAD process, CFD analysis, structure analysis, material selection, and the manufacturing process. His Mechatronics project also includes the final technical report, along with additional information about the robots mechanical and electrical designs. 

-Evidence of Reasoning 
Tyler shows an amazing job of the reasoning behind his decisions. For example, in his surfboard fin project, he compared a "Fat Fin" and "Skinny Fin" using CFD and structural analysis. The results showed that the Skinny Fin provided Better overall solution while also requiring less material. He then selected the Skinny Fin for manufacturing based on the analysis rather than simply choosing a design based on appearance or performance. 

-Professional Tone
Tyler's portfolio also uses technical language and provides specific engineering results instead of only making general claims. His projects include CFD, FEA, manufacturing methods, material properties, and quantitative results. For example, his surfboard fin project reports specific lift, drag, and factors of safety values. THis gives an employer tons of evidence of the engineering projects he has done.
